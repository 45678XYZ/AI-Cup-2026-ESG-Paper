\paragraph{Guaranteed validity and Chinese confirmatory evidence.}
Table~\ref{tab:main-results} compares decision rules on identical base
probabilities and Test rows. Independent argmax (M0) produces invalid tuples
for 12.55\% of document-disjoint predictions; projection and 17-state decoding
(M1--M6) reduce this rate to zero by construction. This zero rate is a
deterministic property of the validity layer, not evidence that the base model
became more accurate. Pooled over both protocols and all three seeds, M0 emits
1{,}527 invalid tuples in 12{,}000 rows, of which 1{,}454 (95.2\%) break one
of two implications: a substantive child under \textsf{PS}=No, or an evidence
quality under \textsf{ES}=No. Table~\ref{tab:invalid-anatomy}(a) gives the
full breakdown, and Table~\ref{tab:invalid-anatomy}(b) derives the 44.3\%
weighted field credit those rows retain. The failure concentrates where a
parent is denied but its children are answered anyway. The $\pm$ values are sample standard deviations across
three seeds, each varying both folds and training, so they describe pipeline
spread, not a confidence interval.

\begingroup\emergencystretch=1em
Table~\ref{tab:contrasts} reports both pre-specified families in full. All
five official wF1 contrasts are undetected after Holm correction. For the
central M1--M0 contrast, $\Delta=-0.001$ with 95\% interval
$[-0.006,0.003]$ and $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=1.000$. This is the absence of a
detected difference; it does not establish a zero effect.

The null is not confined to that contrast. The seven rules span $0.008$ in
official wF1, and that spread is itself one contrast: M5 scores highest and M6
lowest, and $|\textrm{M6}-\textrm{M5}|$ has an interval excluding zero
($[-0.016,-0.001]$) yet does not survive correction
($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=.171$). Even the largest gap between rules is thus below
what this evaluation can resolve.
\par\endgroup

\paragraph{Whole-tuple effects.}
\begingroup\emergencystretch=1em
Exact whole-tuple accuracy tells a different story. M1--M0 is $+0.035$
$[0.028,0.043]$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=.001$. Conversely, 17-state
decoding is not uniformly better: M4--M1 is $-0.006$
$[-0.010,-0.002]$, $p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=.028$. Searching every legal state can
therefore reduce whole-row correctness relative to greedy projection. The
validity guarantee is shared, but the choice among legal tuples still matters.
\par\endgroup

Neither calibration contrast is detected (M3--M2 and M6--M5, on both
pre-specified metrics): fitting biases only on the subset each parent admits
does not improve on global biases. The calibrated projection arms still reach
0.428 and 0.432 tuple accuracy against M1's 0.394, a step no pre-specified
contrast covers.

The post-hoc C-wF1 and hF M1--M0 point estimates are also positive ($+0.004$
and $+0.003$) but do not alter the inferential hierarchy; under same-document
evaluation C-wF1 stays positive at $+0.002$, on an interval $[-0.001,0.005]$
that crosses zero.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.}
\begingroup\emergencystretch=1em
Table~\ref{tab:regimes} compares the two protocols, which target distinct
estimands rather than a biased and an unbiased version of one quantity. Every
rule scores higher under same-document evaluation, by $0.010$ to $0.020$, with
every interval excluding zero. The gap belongs to the split, not to any rule,
and exceeds the $0.008$ separating the rules: which paragraphs are held out
matters more than which decision rule is applied.
\par\endgroup

\paragraph{Multilingual replication.}
Table~\ref{tab:multilingual} fixes one arm per language before inspecting
scores, each under document-disjoint evaluation at $\lambda=0$. Whole-tuple
accuracy rises in all five corpora, whereas weighted macro-F1 rises only for
English and French; Section~\ref{sec:mechanism} reads its two ledger columns.

\begingroup\emergencystretch=1em
Table~\ref{tab:external-arms} reports all four external corpora at both loss
settings, arm by arm. M1--M0 tuple accuracy is positive in $32/32$ arms. The
weighted
macro-F1 contrast is positive in only $14/32$: English $7/8$, French $5/8$,
Japanese $0/8$, and Korean $2/8$. These are within-arm directions, not
evidence about language difficulty: the corpora also differ in source
documents, label distributions, input units, and backbones, and macro-F1
weights each Korean evidence-quality class more heavily
(Section~\ref{sec:metrics}). Projection thus combines a deterministic
validity guarantee with a repeated complete-tuple improvement, while its
field-score effect is not directionally stable across arms.
\par\endgroup

\paragraph{Exploratory robustness.}
\begingroup\emergencystretch=1em
On the Chinese anchor, structural loss lowers M0's invalid-tuple rate from
12.55\% to 5.18\%, with a pre-registered M1 wF1 effect of $+0.00250$
$[-0.00384,0.00860]$ ($p=.427$); it lowers that rate in all 16 external
language-by-backbone pairs, with mixed wF1 and tuple effects. The Chinese
DeBERTa and ELECTRA screens also lack conclusive wF1 gains, and the
same-family base checkpoint's lower invalid rate contradicts a simple
smaller-model mechanism.

\begingroup\emergencystretch=1em
Table~\ref{tab:legality-cost} reports these seven Chinese arms. The
$\lambda=0.3$ arms still emit invalid tuples: the penalty pulls probability
mass toward $\mathcal S$ but, unlike a decision rule, does not confine output
to it. M1--M0 raises tuple accuracy in 7/7 arms and M4--M1 lowers it in 7/7,
so the single-arm M4--M1 result reflects the whole screen.
